Global Warming

Global warming refers to the long-term increase in Earth’s average temperature caused mainly by human activities. The burning of fossil fuels such as coal, oil, and natural gas releases greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide into the atmosphere. These gases trap heat from the sun, creating a “greenhouse effect” that warms the planet.

One major impact of global warming is climate change. Rising temperatures lead to melting glaciers and polar ice caps, causing sea levels to rise. Extreme weather events such as heatwaves, droughts, floods, and stronger storms are becoming more common. Wildlife and ecosystems are also affected, as many species struggle to adapt to changing temperatures and habitats.

Global warming affects human health and daily life as well. Air pollution and heat can increase illnesses, while changes in rainfall can reduce food and water supplies in some regions. Coastal communities are especially vulnerable to flooding caused by rising seas.

To reduce global warming, people and governments can take several actions. Using renewable energy sources like solar and wind power, reducing waste, planting trees, and conserving energy can help lower greenhouse gas emissions. International cooperation is also important to protect the environment for future generations.

Global warming is one of the biggest challenges facing the world today. By taking action now, humanity can help slow climate change and create a healthier, safer planet.
